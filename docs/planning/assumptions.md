# Assumptions and risk register

> **TL;DR:** The highest risks are not a choice of framework: they are whether players will switch
> for this task, whether the content can be published appropriately, and whether recipe conditions
> can be projected without lying. Desk evidence supports hypotheses; it does not validate the product.

## Risk ordering

| Priority | Risk class | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| First | Desirability and publication viability | A correct implementation can still be unwanted or not releasable |
| Next | Content correctness and scope | Fast answers can be confidently wrong |
| Then | Usability and maintenance | People cannot find/trust an answer, or it ages without support |
| Later | Expansion bets | Offline/accounts work consumes effort before need is shown |

Impact and evidence are qualitative. No probability, market size, conversion rate or survey
confidence is invented. Source-linked user/competitor evidence lives in the
[research synthesis](../research/user-research.md) and [benchmark](../research/competitor-benchmark.md).

DS IDs are defined in [archive readiness](../research/data-readiness.md). "Desk research" is
an evidence class, not proof of the criterion. None of A1-A27 is marked confirmed by this phase.
OP IDs are the owner's first-person input ([spec 2.1](../spec.md#21-owner-player-input)):
they settle the owner's intent, not whether other players share the need.
Recruitment must include satisfied current-tool users and players who rarely consult guides,
not only people recruited from complaint threads. Recent-task recall supports problem discovery;
it does not by itself prove willingness to switch or repeated use of this product.

## Concrete risks not settled by a study

- Source/site policy changes: preserve source lineage; never rely on bypassing access controls.
- Wrong/stale recipe corrections: source conflicts must block the affected claim, not silently
  fall back to a plausible recipe.
- Spoiler misclassification: permit report/correction, show explicit reveal scope, and test
  search snippets, aliases, thumbnails and shared links, not just detail paragraphs.
- Content rights and community relationship: attribution does not by itself settle noncommercial,
  trademark, all asset permissions or community expectations.
- Forum sampling: English-language posts overrepresent people who post questions and available
  search results; the sample cannot rank population-wide priorities.
- Progress data loss: browser storage can be cleared or evicted. Mitigate with confirmed writes,
  backup prompts and export, never with silent cloud upload.
- Wrong plans: a plan that combines incompatible conditions wastes a run and erodes trust. Only
  reviewed conditions enter plans; every plan shows reasons and sources.
- Icon dependence: a visual grid needs game art. If icon rights are not cleared, labeled text
  tiles must still meet the task, or the grid stays private/local.
- Misleading numbers: a precise-looking damage figure on a guessed record does more harm than no
  figure. Only reviewed records produce numbers, every number shows its assumptions and version,
  and the lab never ranks builds.
- Patch drift: a balance patch can silently invalidate saved comparisons. Saved builds store
  choices, not results, and show the version they were saved with.

## Change rule

When a check refutes an assumption, update its status and the corresponding O/P/U/flow/DoD row
in the same documentation change. Record inconclusive studies as inconclusive; do not lower a
criterion after observing results merely to label it confirmed.
